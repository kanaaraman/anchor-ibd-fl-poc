ANCHOR-IBD · Proof of concept · NVIDIA DGX Spark + NVIDIA FLARE swarm learning

Predicting biologic response across Swedish and Norwegian IBD registries without moving patient data

Three hospital sites, Region Östergötland and Örebro (SWIBREG) and Oslo University Hospital (NorIBD), each keep their OMOP CDM extract on their own Spark. They train one shared model by passing model weights between themselves, and a different hospital does the averaging each round. No central server holds the data or the model. The outcome is clinical remission at week 52 on the index biologic.

All patients are synthetic. No real registry data was used (consistent with WP2: "no patient data, synthetic examples").

Training replay

Each round, every hospital trains on its own patients. One hospital collects the updates and averages them, then sends the new shared model back. The coordinator only starts the job. NVFlare picks the averaging hospital at random; the order shown here is illustrative.

Federated vs local-only vs pooled

AUROC on new synthetic patients per site who took no part in training, mean of training seeds. "Pooled" puts all data in one place. That is not allowed in reality and is shown only as the ceiling. The axis starts at 0.5, which is chance level. Swarm learning computes the same weighted average as server-based FedAvg, so the federated scores come from the FedAvg simulation.

Sex-disaggregated performance

AUROC by sex on the evaluation patients from all sites. Sex-disaggregated analysis is a core requirement in ANCHOR-IBD.

Privacy vs accuracy

Each site clips its update and adds Gaussian noise before sending it. More noise means less leakage and a lower AUROC.

Per-update Gaussian mechanism only. No formal ε is accounted here; a privacy accountant belongs in the follow-on project.
